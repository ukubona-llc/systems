The color genesis · why six stripes

Wozniak traces it to a sleepless night on the Atari factory floor — watching a black-and-white arcade screen, he realized a one-dollar chip could be made to cycle through color states instead of plain on/off. The first prototype lit up in color in front of Jobs. That's why, the following year, Apple's first real logo carried six stripes instead of one — color wasn't decoration, it was proof the machine could do something no affordable computer had done.

Apple's 1977 rainbow, original order top to bottom: green, yellow, orange, red, purple, blue. Drawn by Rob Janoff, commissioned by Jobs to advertise the Apple II's then-radical color graphics.
